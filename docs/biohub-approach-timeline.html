Biohub competition approach timeline

Chronological decision record through 19 August 2026. The flow map shows dependencies; this view shows when evidence arrived and which paths were closed.

Biohub — Cell Tracking During Development. This competition asks participants to reconstruct a family tree of cells from a sequence of 3D microscope images. It is ongoing; entered 3 July 2026; scheduled finish 29 September 2026. The current public frontier is 0.915, and five submission slots are available on 19 August. Kaggle overview.

What is submitted?

For every movie frame, we submit cell-centre coordinates (nodes) and links from each cell to its likely descendant in the next frame (edges). A cell with two descendants is a predicted division.

What does the score mean?

Higher is better. The main term rewards correct lineage links (adjusted edge Jaccard); a smaller term rewards correct division events. A public score is Kaggle’s result on a hidden subset, while local validation uses labelled training movies.

What is the pipeline?

Find cells in each 3D image → propose links between consecutive frames → select a biologically consistent graph → make conservative repairs. Most experiments change one of these stages rather than replacing the whole system.

How to read the arrows

Each arrow names the experiment that supplied the next baseline or motivated a follow-up test. Red cards are paths closed by evidence; dashed amber cards were intentionally held back rather than submitted.

Where a public notebook or public model asset was used as a base, the relevant node names it. A cited notebook is a source of code or an idea—not evidence that its author’s displayed leaderboard score applies to our run.

3 July — establish a working tracker

Exact DoG + Hungarian

What
Detected bright cell-like spots with classical image processing, then linked each spot to one successor with Hungarian assignment.
Why
Establish a simple end-to-end tracker and a valid submission baseline.
Result
Public 0.827.

NMS 3.8 µm

What
Increased non-maximum suppression, which merges nearby duplicate cell detections, to 3.8 µm.
Why
Test whether reducing duplicate detections improves linking without changing the tracker.
Result
Local 0.8105; public 0.834. Adopted.

U-Net + transformer + ILP

What
Used a learned cell detector and learned link scorer, then an ILP global optimizer to select a consistent lineage graph.
Why
Test a full learned replacement for the classical tracker.
Public base
pilkwang/biohub-tracking-support-pack-50ep-v1 model-support pack.
Result
Public 0.810, below the simpler tracker; closed.

Gap-2 bridges

What
Proposed links across a two-frame absence, inserting a missing intermediate cell position.
Why
Recover lineages when a cell is missed briefly by the detector.
Result
Local validation regressed; deliberately not submitted.
